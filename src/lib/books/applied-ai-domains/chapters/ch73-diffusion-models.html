Chapter 72 ended with two working but imperfect generative mechanisms: VAEs, stable to train but prone to blurry samples, and GANs, capable of sharp samples but through an adversarial game that is genuinely hard to stabilize and diagnose. Neither is a strict improvement on the other. They are different bets with different failure modes. Diffusion models are the field’s answer to both problems at once: a single, stable, non-adversarial training objective that also produces sharp, high-fidelity samples. As of this writing they are the dominant approach behind systems like Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, and DALL-E, and this chapter builds one from the forward-noising process up to classifier-free guidance and latent-space diffusion.

73.1 Motivation

Diffusion flips the generation problem around. Instead of learning “how to produce an image” directly, the task a GAN’s generator or a VAE’s decoder tackles head-on, a diffusion model learns “how to remove one small amount of noise from a slightly-too-noisy image,” a much easier problem, and then chains that one narrow skill hundreds of times, starting from pure noise and ending at a clean image. Nothing about that description mentions an adversarial game or a KL-regularized latent space; it’s a single, well-behaved regression problem, applied repeatedly.

73.2 Exposition

73.2.1 The Forward Process Has a Closed Form

The forward process gradually destroys a real image by adding small amounts of Gaussian noise over many timesteps, according to a fixed, non-learned variance schedule β1,…,βT\beta_1, \dots, \beta_Tβ1​,…,βT​. Given a clean image x0x_0x0​, the noisy version at any timestep ttt can be computed directly, without simulating every intermediate step:

xt=αˉt x0+1−αˉt ϵ,ϵ∼N(0,I)x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_t}\, \epsilon, \qquad \epsilon \sim N(0, I)xt​=αˉt​​x0​+1−αˉt​​ϵ,ϵ∼N(0,I)

where αt=1−βt\alpha_t = 1 - \beta_tαt​=1−βt​ and αˉt=∏s=1tαs\bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s=1}^{t} \alpha_sαˉt​=∏s=1t​αs​ is a cumulative product that shrinks monotonically toward 0 as ttt increases. This closed form is what makes diffusion training simple: a training loop can sample a completely random timestep for each example rather than iterating through the whole chain.

73.2.2 The Network Predicts Noise, Not Images

The reverse process is what actually gets learned: a network ϵθ(xt,t)\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)ϵθ​(xt​,t) is trained to predict, given a noisy image and its timestep, exactly what noise vector was added to produce it. This is a plain regression problem with a single mean-squared-error loss, no adversarial game, no auxiliary regularization term competing against a reconstruction loss the way a VAE’s KL term does. If the network can predict the noise, that noise can simply be subtracted, a little at a time, to walk back toward a clean image.

73.2.3 Reverse Sampling Reconstructs the Image Step by Step

Generation starts from pure noise, xT∼N(0,I)x_T \sim N(0, I)xT​∼N(0,I), and at each timestep going backwards uses the trained network’s noise prediction to compute a slightly-less-noisy version, following directly from inverting the forward process’s algebra:

μt=1αt(xt−βt1−αˉt ϵθ(xt,t)),xt−1={μtt=0μt+βt z,  z∼N(0,I)t>0\mu_t = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\left(x_t - \frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_t}}\, \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\right), \qquad x_{t-1} = \begin{cases} \mu_t & t = 0 \\ \mu_t + \sqrt{\beta_t}\, z, \; z \sim N(0, I) & t > 0 \end{cases}μt​=αt​​1​(xt​−1−αˉt​​βt​​ϵθ​(xt​,t)),xt−1​={μt​μt​+βt​​z,z∼N(0,I)​t=0t>0​

This procedure, DDPM’s ancestral sampling, is nothing more than applying the network’s one narrow skill, “guess the noise,” in a loop, hundreds of times, each time landing a little closer to a plausible clean image.

73.2.4 Diffusion Is Score-Based Generation, Viewed From a Different Angle

The score function of a distribution, ∇xlog⁡p(x)\nabla_x \log p(x)∇x​logp(x), points in the direction that most increases log-density at a given point. Following it moves a point toward more probable, more realistic regions. The trained noise-prediction network is, up to a known scaling factor, already estimating this score:

score(xt,t)≈−ϵθ(xt,t)1−αˉt\text{score}(x_t, t) \approx -\frac{\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)}{\sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_t}}score(xt​,t)≈−1−αˉt​​ϵθ​(xt​,t)​

“Diffusion models” and “score-based generative models” are two names for closely related, in the DDPM formulation, essentially the same, underlying mathematics, not two competing techniques. A single small step along the estimated score, applied to a noisy input, measurably moves it closer to a real clean image, which is precisely what the reverse sampling loop does hundreds of times in sequence.

73.2.5 Classifier-Free Guidance Adds Control Without a Separate Model

A plain diffusion model produces some plausible sample with no control over which one. Classifier-free guidance (CFG) fixes this without training a separate classifier: one network is trained to handle both conditional and unconditional prediction, by randomly replacing the class label with a “null” token during training at some drop probability. At sampling time, the unconditional prediction is extrapolated toward the conditional one, scaled by a guidance_scale:

ϵguided=ϵuncond+s⋅(ϵcond−ϵuncond)\epsilon_{\text{guided}} = \epsilon_{\text{uncond}} + s \cdot (\epsilon_{\text{cond}} - \epsilon_{\text{uncond}})ϵguided​=ϵuncond​+s⋅(ϵcond​−ϵuncond​)

At s=0s = 0s=0 the result is pure unconditional generation, ignoring the class entirely; at s=1s = 1s=1 it is exactly the conditional prediction as trained, with no extrapolation; at s>1s > 1s>1 the conditional signal is amplified beyond what the network directly predicted. No separate classifier ever steers generation. The same network that generates images also carries the conditioning signal, which is exactly the mechanism real text-to-image systems reuse when the conditioning signal is a rich text embedding (Chapter 74) instead of a one-hot class label.

73.2.6 Latent Diffusion Trades Pixel Space for a Compressed Space

Running the full diffusion process, hundreds of network evaluations per sample, directly on raw pixels gets expensive fast at real image resolutions. Latent diffusion trains an autoencoder first, structurally the same idea as Chapter 72’s plain autoencoder, and runs the entire diffusion process in its small latent space instead, decoding back to pixels only once, at the very end. At real image resolutions this compresses something like a 512×512×3512 \times 512 \times 3512×512×3 image down to a 64×64×464 \times 64 \times 464×64×4 latent, well over a hundredfold reduction in dimensionality, which is why Stable-Diffusion-scale models are tractable to train and sample from at all.

73.3 Trade-offs and Limits

Diffusion works well when training stability matters more than sampling speed: a single, well-behaved loss with no adversarial dynamics to manage, and empirically the current state of the art for raw sample fidelity in image, audio, and video generation. It breaks down where sampling speed matters: generation is inherently sequential and expensive, requiring hundreds of network evaluations per sample, versus a GAN’s single forward pass or a VAE’s single decode. Latent diffusion and fast-sampler research mitigate but don’t eliminate this cost. If the requirement is a genuinely fast, one-shot generator, real-time generation, for instance, a GAN, despite its training headaches, still generates in one pass once trained, and that structural difference doesn’t go away just because diffusion produces better samples on average.

73.4 Procedures

Procedure 73.1 (Set Up the Forward Process and a Variance Schedule). When to use: the first step of building any DDPM-style diffusion model.

Common mistake: setting beta_end too high relative to T. alpha_bar at the final timestep collapses to near-zero too fast, and training spends most of its budget on timesteps that are already almost pure noise, wasting capacity on the hardest, least-informative part of the range.

Procedure 73.2 (Train a Noise-Prediction Network, With Optional Class Conditioning). When to use: the core training step; the same function handles both unconditional and classifier-free-guidance-ready conditional models depending on whether label data is supplied.

Diagnostic: if the eps-prediction loss never drops below roughly 1.0, check that the training data is scaled toward the noise schedule’s assumed zero-mean, unit-variance target (this chapter scales pixels to [−1,1][-1, 1][−1,1]) before suspecting the model architecture.

Procedure 73.3 (Reverse (Ancestral) Sampling, With Optional Guidance). When to use: generating a sample once a noise-prediction network is trained.

Common mistake: skipping the “no extra noise at t=0t=0t=0” special case. The final reverse step should be deterministic given the mean; adding noise there produces visibly static-corrupted samples at the very last step.

Procedure 73.4 (Move an Existing Diffusion Model to Latent Space). When to use: pixel-space diffusion works but training or sampling is too slow at the target image resolution.

Common mistake: forgetting to normalize the latent space before diffusing. A plain autoencoder’s raw latent space, unlike a VAE’s, has no reason to be zero-mean/unit-variance, and the diffusion schedule’s math assumes it is.

73.5 Worked Example

The tutorial notebook behind this chapter builds a DDPM-style diffusion model on the same 8x8 digits dataset used in Chapter 72, scaled to [−1,1][-1, 1][−1,1], with T=200T = 200T=200 steps and the linear schedule from Procedure 73.1. The measured schedule values: αˉ0=0.9999\bar\alpha_0 = 0.9999αˉ0​=0.9999 (essentially untouched) decaying to αˉ199=0.1322\bar\alpha_{199} = 0.1322αˉ199​=0.1322 (mostly noise) by the final step.

Training the unconditional noise-prediction network (Procedure 73.2, 150 epochs) produced a loss that fell from roughly 0.98 at initialization (an untrained network guesses noise no better than chance) to a stable plateau around 0.305, with none of the oscillation or instability Chapter 72’s GAN needed active management to avoid. After 200 reverse steps starting from pure noise (Procedure 73.3), the generated samples’ mean and standard deviation landed close to real held-out test data’s own statistics, and a pairwise-distance diversity check, the identical metric Chapter 72 used to catch GAN mode collapse, showed the samples were genuinely varied rather than collapsed onto a handful of shapes.

The score-function relationship was checked directly: a single small step along the estimated score, xt+step_size⋅score(xt,t)x_t + \text{step\_size} \cdot \text{score}(x_t, t)xt​+step_size⋅score(xt​,t), reduced the mean-squared error to a clean image from 0.1230 to 0.1134, real, measured evidence that ϵθ\epsilon_\thetaϵθ​ functions as a score estimator, not just a theoretical claim.

Classifier-free guidance (Procedure 73.3 with label_emb_fn supplied) was trained with drop_prob=0.1 and evaluated against an independent auxiliary classifier (not part of the generative model, used only to score whether generated samples matched the requested digit):

guidance_scale Meaning Fraction of 40 samples matching target digit
0.0 Pure unconditional 0.000
1.0 Conditional prediction as trained, no extrapolation 0.875
3.0 Amplified conditional signal 1.000
7.0 Strongly amplified 1.000

The jump from 0.0 to 0.875 between scales 0 and 1 is the clearest evidence that the conditioning signal is reaching the network at all; the further jump to 1.000 by scale 3 shows guidance doing exactly what the formula predicts. Extrapolating further toward the conditional prediction increases class adherence, up to a point past which (not reached in this toy run) it would trade away diversity and realism instead.

Latent diffusion (Procedure 73.4) trained a 16-dimensional autoencoder latent space first, then ran the same diffusion process there instead of on raw 64-dimensional pixels. The measured effect on model size was substantial: the pixel-space noise-predictor needed 37,184 parameters against the latent-space version’s 8,336, roughly 4.46x fewer parameters, because the latent network only ever has to model a 16-number vector instead of a 64-number image. The measured wall-time gap at this toy scale was small: pixel-space sampling took 0.025s for 16 samples over 200 steps versus 0.023s for latent-space, roughly 1.1x, an honest finding, not a hidden flaw. At 64 versus 16 dimensions with a tiny MLP, both networks are already fast on a CPU, and the dramatic real-world speedup only shows up once the pixel-space alternative is actually large, as it is at real image resolutions.

73.7 How This Connects

This chapter builds directly on Chapter 72 (Generative Foundations): diffusion is best understood as a direct response to VAE blurriness and GAN instability, not an unrelated new idea, and the latent-diffusion procedure reuses Chapter 72’s autoencoder architecture outright. The sinusoidal timestep embedding used throughout this chapter’s noise-prediction network is the same construction as the positional embeddings in this book’s Vision Transformers chapter and in Book 2, Chapter 29 (Training a Transformer Model). This chapter enables Chapter 74 (Multimodal Models & Evaluation): real text-to-image systems replace this chapter’s one-hot class labels with rich text embeddings from a model like CLIP as the conditioning signal, using the exact same classifier-free-guidance mechanism built here. It is often confused with treating “score-based generative models” as a separate technique from diffusion. This chapter’s direct score-function measurement showed they are the same underlying mechanism viewed from two angles.

73.9 Mental Model

A diffusion model never learns to draw a digit. It learns one much narrower skill, given a noisy version of something, guess what noise was added, and generation is nothing more than applying that one narrow skill hundreds of times in a row, starting from static. Everything else in this chapter, guidance, latent space, the score-function connection, is a refinement of how that one skill is trained or applied, not a different skill.
