Systems & Performance is Volume 3, an optional add-on to the core two-volume series. It merges what were originally two separate tracks, GPU kernel programming and CPU-first performance engineering, into one volume, reflecting the dependency between them: the performance-engineering material leans on the GPU-kernel material for its final chapters, once a workload has been shown to genuinely need GPU offload rather than better CPU code.

This volume assumes core Chapter 29 (Training a Transformer Model) and the capacity-estimation material in Chapters 29, 33, and 23.

Reference Rig

Every measured number in this volume (timings, bandwidths, speedups) was taken on one specific machine, not a synthetic or vendor-quoted figure:

NoteReference Rig

NVIDIA RTX 5090 (170 SMs, ~32GB memory, compute capability 12.0, sm_120) + AMD Ryzen 9 5950X, running CUDA 13.3 and PyTorch 2.11.0+cu128.

Numbers pulled from this rig are marked with a footnote reference back to this box (e.g. “14.2 ms†^\dagger†”) rather than repeating the disclaimer at every occurrence. Treat every such number as this machine’s measurement, not a universal constant. The qualitative conclusions (this pattern is faster than that one, this optimisation helps and by roughly this much) generalize far more reliably than the exact figures.

Reproducing These Numbers on Your Own Hardware

Every chapter’s Worked Example section is backed by a companion notebook in book-companion-notebooks that includes the profiling code used to produce its numbers, not just the numbers themselves. Re-running that notebook on your own GPU/CPU is the intended way to get your reference numbers. The point of this volume is the profiling method, not this specific rig’s results. Where a chapter’s conclusion depends on a specific hardware feature (e.g. a particular SM count or cache size), that dependency is called out explicitly so you know when your numbers should diverge from the book’s.
